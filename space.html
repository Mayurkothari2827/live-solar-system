What do these units mean?

A light-year is a distance: how far light travels in one year. A parsec is about 3.26 light-years. An AU is the average Earth–Sun distance, about 150 million km.

Solar masses and radii compare with our Sun. Earth masses and radii compare with Earth. A planet’s period is the length of its year. Eccentricity describes how stretched its orbit is: zero is circular.

What is measured?

Nearby-star and galaxy markers use catalog right ascension, declination and distance. Coordinates are not extrapolated to the present day. Distances have measurement uncertainty.

The Milky Way’s arms and individual model stars, galaxy shapes, exoplanet surface colors and black-hole geometry are reconstructions. Marker sizes are enlarged. Planet sizes and masses from the NASA composite archive include published measurements and model-derived estimates; this snapshot does not include their individual provenance. Stellar colors approximate catalog temperatures where available; they are not observed photographs. No present-day exoplanet orbital phases or axial rotations are claimed.

Deep-space observations reach us after a light-travel delay. This UTC clock is current; these catalogs and telescope images are recorded observations, not a live camera feed.
